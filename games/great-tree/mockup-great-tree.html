The Great Tree — visual mockup

Both halves side by side for comparison; in play each participant sees one panel, all of it. Five fixed channels run through the trunk in each panel, always visible as faint grooves — clicking never creates a channel, it only starts or stops something flowing through one that was already there. Sun always flows down; water always flows up. A small ring at each limb and root — a gate — is where you click.

The complete rulebook

  1. Five limbs, five roots. Crown can hold sun on at most three limbs; Root can run water in at most three roots.
  2. Choosing a fourth automatically releases whichever gate you opened longest ago — nothing to click to let go.
  3. A root is frozen until sun currently falls on the limb it warms. Move the sun away and it re-freezes at once, and any water running in it stops.
  4. Water rising in a root surfaces at the limb it feeds — a different limb from the one that thaws it.
  5. A limb flowers when it has sun and water at once, and stays flowered from then on, freeing both slots. Play starts with one limb already in flower. Four of the five flowering wins.

Neither mapping is ever shown. Flowering is not an action — it happens when the conditions coincide.

CROWN click a gate to move the sun onto that limb
ROOT click a gate to open or close that root

The visual grammar

Two independent channels on every limb, always both showing at once. Sun sets the bud's colour — dull olive when dark, gold when lit. Water sets the bark and the bud's size — bone-pale and small when dry, near-black and glossy with amber sap when watered, and the bud visibly swells. Leaves carry no information; they stay one quiet, muted colour throughout.

Root — three states. Crown sees none of this.

Why this is cheap in React

  • Five fixed channels per panel, always rendered. A channel is a static path; whether it glows and animates is purely a function of current state. Nothing is created or destroyed when a player acts.
  • One consistent gate widget. A small ring at the trunk-limb or trunk-root junction, three visual states (closed/dim, thawed/openable, open/glowing) driven by the same component on both sides — Crown's sun-gates and Root's water-gates are the same shape with different palettes.
  • No labels, no buttons, no release action. Clicking a gate is the only input. A fourth choice auto-drops the oldest of the current three — one rule, no extra control.
  • Two action types. SetSun {limb, lit} and SetFlow {root, open}. Rejections: SunExhausted, WaterExhausted, RootFrozen.
  • Direction is authored, not computed. Every flowing path is drawn in the direction it should appear to travel; a single falling stroke-dashoffset animation makes it move that way. Downward paths get the gold sun treatment, upward paths the blue water treatment — same technique, opposite authoring direction.
